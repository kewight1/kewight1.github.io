Core Practices

A food-first, lifestyle-first framework for feeling and performing at your best.

My approach is simple. Nourish your body with real food. Restore your nervous system with intentional rest, movement, and time outside. The two sides work together. You cannot out-eat chronic stress, and food alone cannot repair a dysregulated nervous system. The practices below are the ones that brought my body back and continue to keep me well.

Nourish

Food is more than nutrition. It is the raw material your body uses to build, repair, and power every cell. What you eat becomes the version of you that shows up for the life you are building.

Core principles

  • Eat whole foods: vegetables, beans, legumes, fruits, grains, meat, poultry, fish, and dairy.
  • Buy local and seasonal when you can.
  • Choose certified organic when possible.
  • Chew slowly (it's good for digestion) and savor each bite.
  • Cook at home more. Eat out less.
  • Listen to your body. Stick to foods that make you feel good.
  • Greens. The most commonly missing food in modern diets. They oxygenate the blood, calm the nervous system, and crowd out less healthy choices.
  • High-quality protein. It steadies your mood, supports muscle, and keeps blood sugar even. Quality matters more than quantity.
  • Healthy fats. They are the building blocks of your brain and nervous system. They also help you feel full and satisfied.
  • Hydration with minerals. Water flushes toxins and fights inflammation. Add a pinch of salt or trace minerals so your cells can actually use what you drink.

Restore

Food can only do so much when your nervous system is stuck in overdrive. The practices below bring the body back to baseline so the food you eat can actually do its work.

Daily Practices

  • Regulate your nervous system. A few slow exhales. A cold splash on the face. Humming or singing for a minute. These small practices stimulate the vagus nerve and shift the body out of fight-or-flight.
  • Move your body. A daily walk is often more useful than an intense workout. Add strength training a few times a week. The goal is not punishment. The goal is a nervous system that can handle what the day asks of it.
  • Get outside. Sunlight, fresh air, and real contact with the earth lower cortisol in measurable ways. Fifteen minutes a day is enough to start.
  • Protect your sleep. Consistent bedtime. A dark cool room. No screens for the last hour. Sleep is where your body actually repairs itself.
  • Nourish your primary foods. At the Institute for Integrative Nutrition, I was taught that food is only one source of nourishment. Relationships, meaningful work, movement, and joy are primary foods. If any of those are starved, no amount of greens will fully restore you.
