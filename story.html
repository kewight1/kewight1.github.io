My Story

How food and lifestyle changes brought me back to myself at 40.

When my body started to break down

I have been interested in wellness my entire life. Reading The Celestine Prophecy in my twenties planted an idea I have never let go of: food is energy. I attended a top nutrition school. I lived at a holistic retreat center for seven months. At 40, my body still started to break down.

It began with small things. Some gut inflammation in the form of silent reflux, a constant lump-in-the-throat sensation and a lot of throat clearing. Nightly back pain. When I look at pictures of myself from that period, I look bloated. I had gone up a clothing size despite walking five miles most days.

By fall of 2022, I was in trouble. Multiple head colds. Chronic asthma. The asthma I had mostly grown out of as a kid was back. My doctor even put me on inhaled steroids.

My mental health was suffering too. I was constantly worrying. When things were at their worst, I could barely function. I was having panic attacks. I felt a heavy, low-grade depression that would not lift. I was scared, and I did not know what was going on. For months I felt terrible: brain fog, anxiety, fatigue, dry coughing.

My doctor was treating symptoms, but no one was treating the cause.

The turning point

It turns out all of the symptoms I had been experiencing were actually downstream of my gut. When the gut microbiome gets out of balance, a long list of seemingly unrelated health issues can show up.

The timing of my symptoms was telling. When I felt at my worst, it was usually after indulging in too many acidic and processed foods and beverages, such as:

  • Non-organic, processed wheat and corn products (crackers, wraps, tortillas, bread, pasta)
  • Cured meats and cheeses
  • Wine
  • Coffee
  • Sparkling water

I know. All the things many of us love. You can have these in limited quantities. I had let my diet go for so long that things had gotten out of control.

Once I started eating differently, things gradually improved. I did not follow a strict protocol or an elimination diet. I made small, consistent changes: more greens, better protein, more water. I paid attention to how foods made me feel and I let my body guide the adjustments.

Within a few months, my body started to come back. The panic attacks quieted. The asthma eased. The brain fog lifted, and I felt like myself again.

The gut-brain connection

What I did not understand at the time is that the gut and the brain talk to each other constantly through the vagus nerve. Roughly ninety percent of the body's serotonin is produced in the gut. When my gut microbiome was out of balance, my mood was too. Better nutrition was one of the pieces that helped me feel like myself again. It was not the only piece. But it was a foundational piece I could control.

A note if you are struggling: If you are experiencing depression, anxiety, or thoughts of self-harm, please reach out to a qualified mental health professional. Food and lifestyle are supportive tools, not replacements for care. In the United States, the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline is available around the clock by calling or texting 988.
